# Cross-Interview Analysis

Read this only when three or more analyzed transcripts are available. Two is not enough.

A single transcript diagnoses one conversation. Patterns across a set diagnose the candidate. This is the only place in the skill where you are permitted to make a claim about the person rather than the interview.

## Preconditions

Do not run this unless all of the following hold:

1. **Three or more transcripts**, each already analyzed independently
2. **Analyzed before comparison.** Never analyze a transcript while holding a hypothesis from earlier ones — that manufactures the pattern you're looking for
3. **Interviews are genuinely comparable.** Different tiers, roles, or interview types are compared with care; a Tier A product-sense round and a Tier C executive round are not the same test

If only two are available, say what the two share and label it a hypothesis to test on the next one. Do not call it a pattern.

## What to compare

Build a matrix: probes down the side, interviews across the top. Populate with lifecycle state and importance.

| Comparison | What a repeat means |
|---|---|
| **Same evidence target unresolved 3+ times** | A genuine capability gap. The strongest finding this skill produces |
| **Same target resolved in one tier, unresolved in another** | Tier-fit problem, not a capability gap — see below |
| **Same candidate failure mode across transcripts** | A habit, not an off day. Coachable and worth naming |
| **Same target unresolved with different interviewers at one company** | Either a real gap or a mis-scoped role. Check the JD |
| **Strong dimensions repeating** | The actual positioning. Under-reported and worth as much as the gaps |

## Tier-fit vs capability gap

The distinction that matters most, and the one candidates get wrong about themselves.

- **Capability gap:** the same evidence target goes unresolved *regardless of tier*. Fix the capability.
- **Tier-fit problem:** resolves cleanly in one tier, stalls in another. The capability exists; the format is the mismatch.

Two common shapes:

- Deep domain candidates clear Tier C on credibility and stall in Tier A on problem framing. They can do the thinking; they don't front-load a point of view in the first ninety seconds because in their working life nobody asks them to.
- Strong framers clear Tier A and stall in Tier C on commercial specifics. They structure well and cannot name a price.

Tier-fit is the more actionable diagnosis, because it points at preparation rather than at a deficiency. Say which one you think it is, and say what would falsify your read.

## Base rates

Before calling anything a pattern, check whether it is one:

- **Is this probe asked everywhere?** A target that appears in every transcript will show more unresolved instances by volume alone. Report the *rate*, not the count.
- **Is the sample biased by tier?** Four Tier C interviews and one Tier A is not evidence about Tier A.
- **Did difficulty escalate?** Later-round transcripts probe harder. Unresolved probes rising over a loop can mean the rounds got harder, not that performance dropped.
- **How much time separates them?** Transcripts six months apart describe two different candidates. Note the gap.

## Output

```markdown
## Cross-Interview Pattern Report
**Corpus:** <n transcripts, date range, tiers, roles>
**Comparability:** strong | mixed | weak — <why>

### Recurring Unresolved Targets
| Evidence target | Asked in | Unresolved in | Rate | Tiers |

### Diagnosis
- **Capability gaps:** <targets unresolved across tiers>
- **Tier-fit problems:** <targets that resolve in one tier only>
- **Recurring failure modes:** <from candidate-assessment.md>
- **Consistent strengths:** <what resolves everywhere — name these>

### Confidence and limits
<Sample size, comparability, base-rate cautions, what would change this read>

### The One Pattern
<Single most consequential recurring finding, stated as a behaviour>
```

## Rules

**Report strengths with the same rigor as gaps.** A candidate who resolves ownership and evidence probes everywhere has a positioning asset, and three interviews is enough to say so. An all-negative pattern report is almost always an artifact of looking only for negatives.

**Never aggregate scores across interviews into a single number.** Different interviewers, rubrics, and difficulty levels. An average of incomparable scores is noise wearing a decimal point.

**One outcome does not validate a pattern.** If a candidate was rejected twice with the same stated feedback, that is corroboration. If they were rejected twice for different stated reasons, the pattern you found may not be what decided either loop.

**Patterns are hypotheses until the next interview.** State what you would expect to see next time if the read is correct. That is what makes it testable rather than a story.
