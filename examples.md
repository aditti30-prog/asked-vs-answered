# Worked Example

Synthetic Tier C transcript. Shortened; turn numbers preserved. Demonstrates a disguised re-ask, an abandoned probe, and — critically — the two engines disagreeing.

## Transcript

**T31 — I1:** So the reconciliation piece. Honestly, three competitors could build it in a quarter. And one of them will walk in and waive the fees for three years just to get the relationship. How do we make the next million dollars off this?

**T32 — Candidate:** I'd think about it as distribution rather than product. We're not selling a reconciliation tool — we're the banking partner, and the tool is one component. Pick a vertical, go deep, build reference stories, and the relationship becomes the thing that's hard to displace. I've seen that play work in a previous role where we moved from a point solution to being the default provider for an entire segment.

**T33 — I1:** Right. Right. Yeah, we've done that in trade. [two turns about a prior deal]

**T41 — I1:** Let me ask you something else. If you took this on — first sixty days, what are you actually doing?

**T42 — Candidate:** Meeting the country teams, understanding regulatory differences across the corridors, mapping where the product is deployed and where it isn't.

**T43 — I1:** Sure. And commercially?

**T44 — Candidate:** Same — understanding which relationships are strongest and building from there.

**T45 — I1:** Okay. [moves to org structure; does not return]

## Demand–Delivery Gap

**Type 1 — strong answer, wrong evidence.** A1 scores well on communication and credibility and poorly on relevance. This is the case the dual-engine design exists for: an answer that sounds good and does not produce what was asked for. A candidate replaying this from memory will remember it as one of their better moments, because it *was* articulate.

Note the two engines disagree, and that disagreement is the finding. Interviewer behaviour at T33 reads as mild acceptance; the answer content clearly misses. Under a single-engine method the acknowledgment token would have masked the miss entirely.

## What this demonstrates

**T41 is invisible turn-by-turn.** Read alone it's a standard first-sixty-days question, adequately answered. Threaded, it's P1 wearing different clothes — and T43 proves it by dropping the disguise. No shared vocabulary, so S1 never fires. The link comes from asking what evidence a complete answer would produce, and noticing it was already requested.

**P2 leaves no trace.** No re-ask, no friction, nothing at that spot. A turn-by-turn reading finds nothing to comment on — while the setup investment says it mattered.

**Ordering.** P2 is reported before P1 in the output despite lower importance and a single ask, because abandoned dominant-dimension probes lead. P1 is `partial`, not `abandoned` — T43 shows I1 still trying.

**Restraint.** Both interpretation confidences are `medium`, and each probe carries a competing explanation. The disciplined read is that P1 and P2 were probably not satisfied — not that either decided the outcome.
